From reference genomes to reusable answers

1000 Genomes supplies the map of correlated variants. Published GWAS summaries supply the evidence about a trait. LDSC connects the two.

One suitable reference can serve many studies. Their summary statistics let us ask new questions without access to the original study participants’ genomes.

How much signal could come from study bias?

The BMI intercept was {{ engine.bmi_intercept }}. The confounding ratio was {{ engine.bmi_ratio }} percentage points, with one standard error.

Under the LDSC model, that ratio assigns about 3.3% of the mean association statistic’s excess over 1 to confounding. The remainder reflects the model’s genetic signal.

Population structure, relatedness, reference choice, and study corrections affect this interpretation. The intercept helps separate these effects from many small genetic contributions.

Which traits share genetic influences?

Cross-trait LDSC uses two GWAS summaries and an appropriate LD reference to estimate genetic correlation. It can compare metabolic, psychiatric, and other traits.

A positive correlation indicates shared genetic influences in the same direction. A negative correlation indicates opposing directions. Neither result alone establishes causality.

The original cross-trait paper explains the method. This BMI example contains one trait and does not calculate a cross-trait result.
